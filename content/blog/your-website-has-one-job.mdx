Most small business websites look fine and do nothing. They have an about page, a photo gallery, a contact form buried at the bottom, and no clear reason for a visitor to pick up the phone.

A local business website has one job: turn the person who lands on it into a call, a booking, or a quote request. Everything on the page either helps with that or gets in the way.

## Say what you do and where, on the first screen

A visitor decides in seconds whether they are in the right place. The first thing they see should answer two questions: what do you do, and do you do it near me. "Emergency plumbing in north Dallas" beats "Quality you can trust" every time.

## Make the next step obvious

- A tap-to-call button that stays visible on a phone
- A booking or quote form that asks only what you need to get back to them
- The same call to action repeated down the page, so nobody has to scroll back up to find it

## One page per service

Someone searching for "water heater replacement" wants a page about water heater replacement, not a list of twelve services. A page for each service gives Google something specific to rank, and gives the visitor a page that matches what they searched for.

## Fast on a phone

Most local searches happen on a phone, often in a hurry. A site that is slow to load loses people before they read a word. Speed is not a technical detail. It is part of the sales pitch.

## Put the proof where the decision happens

Reviews, photos of real work, and the areas you serve belong next to the call to action, not on a separate page nobody opens.
