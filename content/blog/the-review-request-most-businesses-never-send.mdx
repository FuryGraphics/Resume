Before anyone calls you, they read your reviews. Most local businesses have far fewer than their happy customers would give them, and the reason is simple: nobody asked.

## Why it doesn't happen

You finish the job, the customer is pleased, and you move on to the next one. Asking for a review means remembering to, finding the link, and sending it while the job is still fresh. On a busy week, that never happens.

## Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is right after the job is done, while the customer is still pleased with it. A text or email that arrives then, with one tap through to your Google review form, gets answered. One that arrives two weeks later does not.

## Make it one tap

Every extra step loses people. The request should take them straight to your Google profile with the review box open, not to your homepage or a page asking them to search for you.

## Hear the problems first

A good funnel gives an unhappy customer a private way to reach you before they post anything. You get the chance to fix it, and you hear about the problem from them instead of from a stranger reading it.

## What I set up

Every finished job triggers a review request by text and email. Happy customers are sent straight to your Google profile. Your rating climbs on its own, and you can watch it on a dashboard instead of wondering.
