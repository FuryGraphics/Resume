Replying to reviews feels like admin. It is actually marketing. Every reply is read by two people: the customer who wrote the review, and every future customer scrolling through your profile deciding whether to call you.

Write for the second person.

## Reply to every review

Not just the bad ones. A short, specific thank you under a five-star review shows a prospect that there is a real person behind the business, and that customers get noticed.

## Keep the good ones short and specific

"Thanks, Maria. Glad the new water heater is working, and that we got to you before the weekend." beats "Thank you for your business!" pasted under every review. Mention the job, not just the name.

## Handle the bad ones calmly

- Reply within a day or two, not in the heat of the moment
- Thank them for the feedback, even when it stings
- Acknowledge the problem without arguing the details in public
- Take it offline: give a name and a direct way to reach you
- If you fixed it, say so briefly

A calm, professional reply to a one-star review can do more for your reputation than another five-star review. Prospects are not looking for a perfect record. They are looking for how you behave when something goes wrong.

## Never do this

- Argue with the customer or blame them in public
- Share anything private about the job or the customer
- Offer a discount or a gift in exchange for changing a review
- Paste the same line under every review

## Make it a habit, not a project

The businesses with the best profiles are not the ones that sit down once a quarter to catch up. They reply as reviews come in.
